Career Reflection

Twenty years of building systems that hold — and leading the people who run them

I did not set out to become a program manager. I set out to understand how work holds together. My first role, in 2004, was as an internal systems business analyst for Quality Management Systems — walking into more than twenty client organizations, mapping how they actually worked against how their manuals said they worked, and closing the distance between the two. That distance, I learned, is where cost hides, where risk lives, and where trust is either built or lost. Twenty years later, I am still closing that distance. Only the scale has changed.

“If you cannot show how a thing was verified, you do not yet know that it works.”

Standards taught me a discipline I have never put down: if you cannot show how a thing was verified, you do not yet know that it works. Agility and compliance are not enemies; they are two names for knowing, at every step, where you stand.

Transformation is a quality system for change. It succeeds when the cadence is real and fails when it is theatre. I am a servant-leader with a quality-systems spine, a coach who can also prove it in an audit. What I bring next is what I have always brought, at larger scale and with better questions: execution you can inspect, leadership you can trust, and transformation that leaves evidence behind it.
